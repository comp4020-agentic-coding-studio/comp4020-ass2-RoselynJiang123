# Process overview

## What I built

SLOP8814 *Designing the Sham* is a postgraduate course about one object, the placebo operation, and one instrument I invented for it, the Sham Fidelity Ladder. The student builds a sham-controlled protocol one section per week, so by Week 11 the protocol exists and Week 12 is its defence.

## How I got here

In Assignment 1 I built one page, and the risk was a broken page. With twenty-odd pages, a single page can be fine while the course is wrong: two pages can disagree. The brief's own warning, "twelve weeks that repeat one another", is also a relationship between pages, not a property of any one of them. So my first decision was what a good course is. My answer: one idea held all semester, visible progression, assessment that grows out of the weeks, and no claim the course cannot source. I asked the agent to encode that before it wrote any content.

I split the work. A planning session wrote the harness and every word of copy. A build session in the repo only moved that copy into the site. Otherwise twelve weeks would be drafted twelve times in twelve registers. [`423e0bf`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-RoselynJiang123/commit/423e0bfa46ff70e5307f56f1197d256e7981ed27) is that harness: `CLAUDE.md` holds six course rules, and `COURSE-SPEC.md` is the single source of truth, marked "final copy: move it, do not rewrite it". It builds on the working rules I carried over from week 6 ([`302f9f1`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-RoselynJiang123/commit/302f9f17062423b64178e29a1991e5b8f0d476fc)).

The key architectural call was to make the course's promises machine-readable. Every Round carries custom frontmatter (`week`, `prereqs`, `usesLadder`, `protocolSection`, `trials`), which the build passes into the course API. `spec/course-promises.test.ts` then reads the course like a timetable. Its checks are that the Ladder is never used before the Week 4 lecture defines it, that the consent workshop precedes the protocol that needs a consent form, that A3 falls at least seven days after the A2 it reviews, and that every Round adds a different protocol section. That last one is my answer to "weeks that repeat": it cannot judge whether two weeks feel alike, but it can refuse two weeks that do the same job. The trial registry check comes from the subject itself. In a course about shams, an invented citation is a sham, so content may name only registered trials.

To know the tests tested something, I had the agent run them against the starter before writing content. The failures are saved in [`docs/process/spec-first-run.txt`](docs/process/spec-first-run.txt), and they turned green in [`373bde9`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-RoselynJiang123/commit/373bde9567bfa64dc9826bcf2a6bd2668cf50be0).

What I deliberately left out of the harness was voice and coherence. The banned-phrase list is a floor, not a voice. Whether Week 7 is compelling is my judgement, so `CLAUDE.md` says so explicitly instead of pretending a test could decide it.

Two moments showed where each kind of check belongs. The spec said SLOP8982, but the repo arrived with 814. The agent followed the `CLAUDE.md` rule to keep the repo's digits rather than the spec text ([`d3b87bf`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-RoselynJiang123/commit/d3b87bfd466f9b1ee0269ae3d9f430ea278bd004)), and I corrected the spec and social card afterwards ([`1ff1f7f`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-RoselynJiang123/commit/1ff1f7faf558cf46cd6111b38b00440ead75150d)). When a harness rule and a content document conflict, the rule should win, and here it did. In [`89d571d`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-RoselynJiang123/commit/89d571d3101fc0edb4c4e067e83942771c500c65) every check was green, but the screenshots at 1920×1080 and 390×844 showed the course colours missing from the home and Round pages, because they bypassed the local layout. No automated check could see that. Looking at both viewports did, and that is why I kept that judgement human.
