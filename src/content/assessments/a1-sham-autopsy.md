An autopsy is performed after the outcome is known, to find out what actually happened. In this assessment you take apart one published sham-controlled trial and establish, from the paper alone, what its sham did, what its patients could have detected, and what the trial was therefore entitled to conclude.

## Choosing your trial

Choose one trial from the approved list below. The five landmark trials are excluded, because we took them apart together.

- Cobb et al. (1959) or Dimond et al. (1960) — internal mammary artery ligation
- FIDELITY (Sihvonen et al., 2013) — arthroscopic partial meniscectomy
- CSAW (Beard et al., 2018) — subacromial decompression
- VAPOUR (Clark et al., 2016) — vertebroplasty in acute fractures
- VERTOS IV (Firanescu et al., 2018) — vertebroplasty
- SPYRAL HTN-OFF MED (Böhm et al., 2020) — renal denervation
- ORBITA-2 (Rajkumar et al., 2023) — PCI

Any other published sham-controlled trial of a procedure needs the convenor's agreement by the end of Week 6.

## What you submit

**1. The rating sheet (one page).** Use this structure:

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| Trial | Citation, procedure, population |
| Rung | The Ladder rung you assign |
| Evidence | For each element of that rung: the element, and the sentence in the paper showing it was present |
| The missing element | The first element of the rung above that the trial did not achieve, with the evidence |
| Blinding channels | Patient, operator, assessor, analyst: blinded, not blinded, or not reported |
| Confidence | High, moderate or low, with one sentence of reason |

**2. The essay (1,200 words).** It must:

- defend your rating against the most plausible alternative rung;
- analyse all four blinding channels, including any evidence of unblinding;
- state what the trial could conclude because of how its sham was built, and what it could not;
- say what you would change in the sham, and what that change would cost the patient.

## Marking criteria

| Criterion | Marks | High Distinction (80+) | Distinction–Credit (60–79) | Pass (50–59) | Not yet (<50) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rating and its defence | 40 | Every element evidenced from the paper; the strongest alternative rung is addressed and answered | Rating evidenced; alternatives mentioned but not fully answered | Rating stated with some evidence; gaps in the element list | Rating asserted without evidence, or misapplies the Ladder |
| Blinding analysis | 25 | All four channels analysed; leaks identified and their likely effect on the result estimated | All four channels covered; leaks noted | Patient blinding discussed; other channels thin | Blinding treated as a single yes/no |
| What the trial could and could not conclude | 25 | Conclusions tied directly to specific features of the sham; overreach in the paper identified | Sound link between design and conclusions | Conclusions restated from the abstract with limited analysis | Conclusions unrelated to the design |
| Writing | 10 | Precise, economical, every claim sourced | Clear with minor lapses | Understandable; some imprecision | Hard to follow; unsourced claims |

## Before you submit

- My trial is on the approved list, or I have the convenor's agreement in writing.
- Every element on my rating sheet points to a sentence in the paper.
- I have analysed all four blinding channels.
- Every reference is to a paper I have opened.

## Questions

**Can I rate a trial at a rung between two rungs?** No. Choose the lower rung and explain what it nearly achieved.

**Can my rating disagree with the published authors' description of their sham?** Yes. That is often where the best essays start.

**Can I use my notes from the Week 4 disagreement log?** Yes, and you should.
