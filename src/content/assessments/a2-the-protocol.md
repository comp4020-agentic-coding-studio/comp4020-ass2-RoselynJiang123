This is the course. You have been writing it since Week 1: one section per Round, in your protocol log. For A2 you revise those sections into a single protocol for a sham-controlled trial of a procedure that is performed today without one.

The standard is simple to state and hard to meet: a theatre team should be able to perform your sham from your text, and an ethics committee should be able to decide on it from your text.

## Structure

| Section | From Round | What it must contain | Guide length |
|---|---|---|---|
| §1 The procedure and its claim | 1 | Population, procedure, outcome, timepoint; why it has not been tested | 200 |
| §2 The threats list | 2 | Non-specific effects, ranked, with how each is held constant | 300 |
| §3 Precedent | 3 | The closest published trial and what you do differently | 250 |
| §4 Fidelity level | 4 | Your rung, every element of it, and why not one higher | 300 |
| **The sham, as performed** | 4 | Step by step, at the level of an operation note: anaesthesia, positioning, what is done, what is said, duration, dressing, recovery | 600 |
| §5 Blinding plan | 5 | Four channels, leak audit table, allocation guesses | 400 |
| §6 Primary outcome and sample size | 6 | Outcome, assessor, timepoint, calculation with sourced assumptions | 400 |
| §7 Risk justification | 7 | Risks added by your rung; the answer to Macklin | 400 |
| §8 Consent form | 8 | Full text (not counted); one paragraph on what the read-aloud test changed | 100 |
| §9 Stopping rules and post-trial access | 9 | Harm, benefit, futility; the committee; unblinding; what the sham arm receives | 400 |
| §10 What would change practice | 10 | Pre-registered interpretation of each result; who needs to hear it | 350 |
| §11 Limits statement | 11 | What your sham cannot rule out | 300 |

## Marking criteria

| Criterion | Marks | High Distinction (80+) | Distinction–Credit (60–79) | Pass (50–59) | Not yet (<50) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| The sham, as specified | 30 | A team could perform it from the text; every element of the stated rung is present; nothing in it would unblind the patient | Performable with minor gaps; rung justified | Recognisable sham, but key steps or elements missing | Sham described in general terms only |
| Blinding plan | 15 | All four channels; realistic leaks with specific countermeasures; guesses planned and interpreted | All channels with plausible countermeasures | Patient blinding covered; others thin | Blinding asserted, not planned |
| Outcome and sample size | 15 | Outcome, assessor and timepoint justified against alternatives; calculation correct with sourced assumptions | Justified and correct with minor gaps | Calculation present with unsourced assumptions | Missing or incorrect |
| Risk justification | 15 | Risks of the rung listed specifically; strongest objection answered directly; lower rung considered and rejected for stated reasons | Risks listed and argued | Risks listed without argument | Risks minimised or omitted |
| Consent form | 15 | Contains all seven required elements; plain language; describes the arms identically where they are the same; read-aloud test applied | All elements; minor clarity issues | Most elements; some sentences would unblind or alarm | Missing required elements |
| Stopping rules and limits | 10 | Pre-specified rules for harm, benefit and futility; honest limits that anticipate reviewers | Rules and limits present | Rules vague; limits generic | Missing |

## Rules

- Your procedure must be the one approved in Week 1, unless the convenor agreed a change.
- You may not propose L0.
- You may use tables. You may not use clinical images.
- Every reference must be to a paper you have opened.

## Before you submit

- Could a theatre team perform my sham from my text alone?
- Does anything in my consent form describe the two arms differently where they are the same?
- Have I stated what the sham arm is offered at the end, and when?
- Is every assumption in my sample size sourced?
- Have I written the limits a reviewer would raise before they raise them?

## Questions

**Can I change my rung after Week 4?** Yes. Say why in §4. A changed mind with a reason is worth more than a consistent one without.

**What if I find a published sham-controlled trial of my procedure?** Tell the convenor. Usually you will keep the procedure and treat that trial as your precedent.

**Who reads my protocol?** The marker, and one classmate, who reviews it as a member of the ethics committee for A3.
