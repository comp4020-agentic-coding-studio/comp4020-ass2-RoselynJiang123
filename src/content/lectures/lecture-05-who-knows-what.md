## Summary

"Double-blind" is too vague to design with. This lecture separates blinding into four channels — patient, operator, outcome assessor and analyst — and argues that in surgery the operator is the permanent leak. The design task is to stop what the operator knows from reaching the other three.

## Outline

1. **Four channels.** Who could know the allocation, and when they could find out.
2. **Why the operator is the leak.** Someone has to know whether to do the operation. Everyone they talk to afterwards is at risk.
3. **Auditory isolation in ORBITA.** Sedation protected one thing; headphones protected another. What each was for.
4. **Asking patients to guess.** Blinding indices, when to collect guesses, and what to do if most patients guess correctly.
5. **What the Ladder cannot do.** A rung protects the patient's channel only. The other three are protected by logistics.
