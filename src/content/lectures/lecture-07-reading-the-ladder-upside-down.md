## Summary

To a methodologist the Ladder climbs towards better evidence. To an ethics committee it climbs towards more risk, offered to someone who cannot benefit. This lecture sets out when that exchange can be justified, using the argument that surrounded the sham-controlled trials of cell transplantation for Parkinson's disease.

## Outline

1. **Equipoise.** Genuine uncertainty within the expert community, and why a surgeon's personal confidence does not count against it.
2. **Risk to someone who cannot benefit.** The feature that makes sham surgery different from a sugar pill.
3. **Macklin, Freeman, Horng and Miller.** The objection, the defence, and the conditions that emerged from the debate.
4. **The burr hole in Freed et al. (2001).** Partial-thickness holes in the skull: what they risked, and what they bought.
5. **Reading the Ladder from the committee's side of the table.** Each rung, restated as the risk it adds.

## Read alongside

Macklin (1999) · Freeman et al. (1999) · Horng & Miller (2002) · Freed et al. (2001)

## The question to leave with

What is the most your sham arm could lose, and what is the least your trial could learn?
