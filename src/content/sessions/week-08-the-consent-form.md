## The claim

The consent form is the only part of the protocol the patient reads. It must say plainly that they may receive a procedure with no active element. It must say it without teaching them how to work out which arm they are in.

Most failed sentences fail in one of two ways. They are too frightening to sign ("you may undergo a fake operation"), or they are too detailed to stay blind ("if you receive the sham, you will not hear the drill"). A good consent form describes both arms in the same words wherever the arms are the same, and in plain words wherever they differ.

## What the form must contain

1. That the study compares the procedure with a sham procedure, and what "sham" means here.
2. What will physically happen in the sham arm, including anaesthesia, wounds and scars.
3. The risks of the sham procedure itself, not only of the real one.
4. That neither the patient nor the people assessing them will be told the allocation until a stated point.
5. What happens at the end: whether the sham arm is offered the real procedure, and when.
6. That the patient can withdraw at any time without affecting their care.
7. Who to contact, and how emergency unblinding works.

## Before the Round

- Read the consent and crossover arrangements described in **Kallmes et al. (2009)**. Consider what the option to cross over after one month offered patients, and what it might have done to blinding.

## In the room

**Sentence clinic.** Each draft is read aloud to someone from outside the course, who then explains back what they agreed to. We mark the gap between the two.

| Failing sentence | What goes wrong | Better |
|---|---|---|
| "You may receive a fake surgery." | Frightening, and "fake" implies trickery | "Half of the people in this study will have a sham procedure. This means…" |
| "The placebo group will not have the cement injected." | Tells the patient what to listen and smell for | "Both procedures take about the same time and happen in the same room." |
| "There is no risk in the sham arm." | False | "The sham procedure has its own risks, which are…" |
| "You will be told your group at the end." | "The end" is undefined | "You will be told which procedure you had after your twelve-month visit." |

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0:00 | Pairs swap drafts and mark every sentence that fails either test. |
| 0:30 | Read-aloud test with a volunteer from outside the course. |
| 1:00 | Rewrite. Check reading level with any standard readability tool. Aim for plain language a typical adult can read without help. |

## Key terms

- **Informed consent.** Agreement given with an understanding of what will happen, its risks and the alternatives.
- **Authorised deception.** Agreeing in advance not to be told something — here, the allocation.
- **Crossover.** Offering patients in one arm the other arm's treatment at a stated point.
- **Emergency unblinding.** The procedure for revealing a patient's allocation when their care requires it.
