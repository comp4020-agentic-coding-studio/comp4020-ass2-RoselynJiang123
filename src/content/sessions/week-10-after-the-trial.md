## The claim

A negative sham-controlled trial ends a question for methodologists. It does not always end the procedure.

After the two vertebroplasty trials of 2009, guidance turned against the procedure. Then VAPOUR (2016) reported benefit in patients with acute fractures, and VERTOS IV (2018) did not. Knee arthroscopy for degenerative disease followed a longer path: from Moseley (2002), through FIDELITY (2013), to a strong recommendation against it in the 2017 BMJ Rapid Recommendation.

In both cases practice moved more slowly than the evidence. The reasons were ordinary, and a protocol can anticipate them.

## Why practice resists

| Reason | What it sounds like | What a protocol can do in advance |
|---|---|---|
| Payment | The procedure is funded and the alternative is not | Name the funders and guideline bodies who will receive the result |
| Training | Surgeons are skilled in it and trainees need cases | Consult the relevant college at the design stage |
| Demand | Patients ask for it by name | Plan the patient-facing summary before the result is known |
| The wrong patients | "The trial studied the wrong people" | Define the population with its critics before enrolment |
| The wrong technique | "That is not how we do it" | Have the technique specified by its advocates |

The last two are the most important. A trial that its critics helped to design is harder to dismiss.

## Before the Round

- Read **VAPOUR (Clark et al., 2016)** and **VERTOS IV (Firanescu et al., 2018)** side by side. List every difference in who was enrolled and how the sham was done.
- Read the **2017 BMJ Rapid Recommendation** on knee arthroscopy (Siemieniuk et al.). Note how it describes the strength of its recommendation.

## Key terms

- **Evidence–practice gap.** The distance between what trials show and what clinicians do.
- **Pre-registered interpretation.** A statement, made before results are known, of what each possible result will be taken to mean.
- **Subgroup argument.** The claim that a null trial missed a group of patients who do benefit.

## Protocol log — §10 What would change practice

State the result that would make you stop recommending the procedure, the result that would make you recommend it more widely, and who would need to hear each. Name the critics you would invite onto the steering committee. Up to 350 words.
