## The claim

Every sham is a list of things the patient must not be able to tell. The **Sham Fidelity Ladder** orders that list into six rungs, from being told (L0) to being unable to tell (L5). The Ladder is a teaching instrument invented for this course, not a published standard.

It is measured **from the patient's side of the drape**. What the surgeon does behind the drape matters only if the patient can see it, hear it, smell it, feel it, or find it afterwards.

Every rung up buys blinding with risk. This Round introduces the instrument; every Round after it uses it.

## The Ladder

| Rung | Name | What the sham includes |
|---|---|---|
| L0 | Told | The patient is told a procedure took place. Nothing else happens. It is here so the Ladder has a floor. You will not propose it. |
| L1 | Staged | The room, the gown, the monitoring, the drapes, the waiting. No skin is breached. |
| L2 | Breached | Local anaesthetic, a puncture or a skin incision. Nothing goes deeper. |
| L3 | Approached | Instruments travel towards the target and stop short of treating it. |
| L4 | Mimicked | L3, plus the sensory signature of the real procedure: its sounds, smells, pressures and duration. |
| L5 | Indistinguishable | L4, plus matched anaesthesia, matched recovery, matched dressing and scar, and a team whose behaviour afterwards has been scripted as carefully as the procedure itself. |

**Rating rules.**

1. A trial sits on the highest rung whose every element it achieves. One missing element drops it a rung.
2. Every element must be evidenced by a sentence in the paper. If the paper does not say it, it did not happen.
3. Rate what the patient could detect, not what the investigators intended.
4. A rating is an argument. Disagreement is recorded, not averaged.

## Before the Round

Attend the lecture or work through the [slides](/decks/week-04-fidelity-ladder/). Bring your Week 3 trial table.

## In the room

**Rate the five.**

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0:00 | Each table places all five landmark trials on the Ladder independently, with one evidence sentence per element. |
| 0:40 | Tables reveal their ratings together. Where tables disagree, each defends its rung for two minutes. |
| 1:10 | Disagreements go into the **disagreement log**: trial, rungs proposed, the element in dispute, the sentence each side relied on. They are not resolved. They are the raw material for A1. |
| 1:25 | Write §4. |

The course's provisional ratings — Buchbinder 2009 at L4, ORBITA at L5 — are teaching positions, not published facts. You are expected to argue with them.

## Key terms

- **Fidelity.** How closely the sham reproduces the patient's experience of the real procedure.
- **Rung.** A level of fidelity on the Ladder. Each rung contains all the rungs below it.
- **Element.** One thing a rung requires: an incision, a sound, a matched duration.
- **Disagreement log.** The record of rating disputes kept from this Round onwards.

## Protocol log — §4 Fidelity level

State the rung you will build to, list every element of that rung as it applies to your procedure, and give the reason you are not building one rung higher. Up to 300 words.

- **A strong §4** can say what the patient would notice if the sham were one rung lower.
- **A weak §4** chooses L5 because it is the top.

## Check yourself

1. Why is the Ladder measured from the patient's side of the drape and not the surgeon's?
2. What is the cheapest element that would move your design up one rung?
3. What does that element cost the patient?
