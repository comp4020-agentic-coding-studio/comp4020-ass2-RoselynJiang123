## The claim

The five landmark trials look like five different specialties: orthopaedics, interventional radiology, cardiology, nephrology and hypertension, neurosurgery. Read together, they share one structure.

Each tested a procedure that was already in wide use. Each procedure had a plausible mechanism and enthusiastic practitioners. Each trial built a sham arm designed to carry the ritual of the operation without its mechanism. And each produced a result that the field then had to live with, whether or not it wanted to.

The shape is the lesson. The specialty is detail. Once you can see the shape, you can see where your own procedure sits in it.

## Before the Round

Read the abstract and methods of all five. Read the description of the sham procedure in each one twice: once for what was done, once for what the patient would have noticed.

- **Moseley JB et al., NEJM 2002** — arthroscopic lavage or debridement for knee osteoarthritis.
- **Buchbinder R et al., NEJM 2009** and **Kallmes DF et al., NEJM 2009** — vertebroplasty for osteoporotic vertebral fractures. Two trials, published together, counted here as one landmark.
- **Al-Lamee R et al., Lancet 2018 (ORBITA)** — PCI for stable angina.
- **Bhatt DL et al., NEJM 2014 (SYMPLICITY HTN-3)** — renal denervation for resistant hypertension.
- **Freed CR et al., NEJM 2001** — embryonic dopamine-neuron transplantation for Parkinson's disease.

## In the room

**The trial table.** Each table takes one trial and fills the same eight columns. The five rows go on one wall.

| Procedure | Participants | What the sham did | Who was blinded | Primary outcome | Timepoint | Result | What happened next |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| *Example:* arthroscopic lavage or debridement (Moseley 2002) | Adults with knee osteoarthritis | Skin incisions and a simulated procedure; no arthroscope inserted | Patients and outcome assessors | Pain and function | Up to two years | No difference between arms | Weeks 10 and 11 |

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0:00 | Tables fill their row from the paper, not from memory. Every cell must point to a sentence. |
| 0:40 | The five rows go up. We read **across the columns**, not down the rows. What does every "what the sham did" cell have in common? |
| 1:10 | Each person finds the trial closest to their own procedure and writes §3. |

## Key terms

- **Landmark trial.** In this course, a trial whose sham arm changed how a field argued about its own procedure.
- **Null result.** No detectable difference between arms. Not the same as proof that there is no difference.
- **Precedent.** A published trial whose design your own protocol borrows from and can be judged against.

## Protocol log — §3 Precedent

Name the published trial your design most resembles, and one thing you will do differently. Explain the difference in terms of your procedure, not in terms of the precedent's faults. Up to 250 words.

Also record your search: where you looked for an existing sham-controlled trial of your own procedure, and what you found. If you find one, talk to the convenor this week.

## Check yourself

1. In which of the five trials could the patient most easily have guessed their allocation?
2. Which trial's sham came closest to the real procedure?
3. Pick one "what happened next" cell. Why did practice move, or not?
