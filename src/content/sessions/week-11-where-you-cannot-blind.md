## The claim

Some procedures have no upper rungs. You cannot sham a transplant, an amputation, a trauma laparotomy, or a procedure whose effect the patient can see in the mirror. Devices raise a different problem.

SYMPLICITY HTN-3 was negative. A redesigned approach to renal denervation was tested again under sham control in SPYRAL HTN-OFF MED, and this time blood pressure fell. A sham trial tests one device, in one set of hands, at one point in its development. The device keeps moving. So does the argument about which version the trial really tested.

Where a full sham is impossible, there are alternatives. Each keeps something and gives something up. The skill is in knowing exactly what.

## The alternatives

| Design | What it keeps | What it gives up |
|---|---|---|
| Partial sham (e.g. diagnostic procedure only) | Some of the ritual; patient blinding for part of the experience | The parts of the procedure that cannot be imitated |
| Three-arm trial with a no-treatment arm (as in CSAW) | A measure of how much the ritual alone does | Blinding in the no-treatment arm |
| Blinded outcome assessment only | Assessor and analyst channels | Patient blinding; expectation stays uncontrolled |
| Expertise-based trial | Each surgeon does only what they believe in | Patient blinding; surgeon effects mixed with procedure effects |
| Registry and follow-up studies | Scale and real-world practice | Randomisation and blinding |

## Before the Round

- Read the design of **CSAW (Beard et al., 2018)**: an investigational arthroscopy arm alongside a no-treatment arm. Ask what the no-treatment arm added that a two-arm sham trial could not.
- Read **SPYRAL HTN-OFF MED (Böhm et al., 2020)**. List what changed since SYMPLICITY HTN-3: the device, the patients, the procedure, or the measurement.

## In the room

**Break it.** Each protocol is handed to another table with one instruction: find what this sham cannot rule out.

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0:00 | Tables read one protocol each and list everything its sham cannot rule out. |
| 0:40 | The list goes back to the author, who marks each item: *addressed*, *acknowledged*, or *new to me*. |
| 1:00 | Everything marked *new to me* goes into §11. |

## Key terms

- **Partial sham.** A sham that reproduces only part of the procedure, usually its diagnostic step.
- **Expertise-based trial.** A trial in which each surgeon performs only the procedure they are expert in.
- **Learning curve.** Improvement in outcomes as operators gain experience; a problem when a trial runs early in it.
- **Device iteration.** Changes to a device's design between trials, which make earlier results harder to apply.

## Protocol log — §11 Limits statement

State what your sham cannot rule out, before a reviewer states it for you. Include at least one limit from the "break it" exercise. Up to 300 words.
