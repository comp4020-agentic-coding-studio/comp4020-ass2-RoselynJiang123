## The claim

Everything in your protocol has been written by someone who wants the trial to happen. This Round gives it to people who do not have to.

A sham protocol is finished when someone who wants to stop it cannot.

## How the defence runs

Each protocol goes before a panel of three classmates sitting as the Ethics Committee, with the convenor in the chair.

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 10 min | The author presents: the procedure and its claim, the rung, the blinding plan, and the risk justification. No slides are required. |
| 15 min | Questions from the panel. The author answers. |
| 5 min | The panel confers in private. It does not announce a decision. That is A3. |

**Panel roles.** Each panel has three members.

- **The methodologist** asks whether the trial can answer its question.
- **The lay member** asks whether a patient would understand what they are agreeing to.
- **The sceptic** argues that the trial should not run at all.

## Questions the panel may ask

- Why this rung and not the one below it?
- What will the sham arm remember about the day of the procedure?
- Who, in your pathway, is most likely to unblind a patient by accident?
- What result would make you stop doing this procedure?
- What does the sham arm get at the end, and when?
- If this trial is negative, who will not believe it, and why?

## Key terms

- **Crit.** A structured review in which work is presented and questioned by peers.
- **Conditions of approval.** Changes a committee requires before a study may proceed.

## Protocol log — §12 Defence notes

Write down the three questions you expected, the three you were asked, and the difference between the two lists. Up to 200 words. This section is not part of A2. It is for you, and for the next protocol you write.

## Check yourself

1. Which question were you least prepared for?
2. Did any panel member change your mind about your rung?
3. What would you change before submitting this protocol to a real committee?
