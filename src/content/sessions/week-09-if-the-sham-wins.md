## The claim

A trial with a sham arm must agree, before it starts, when it will stop, who watches the interim data, when a patient is unblinded, and what the sham arm is offered at the end. These decisions are hardest to make once the answer is visible. That is why they are written down first.

The uncomfortable case deserves its own sentence. The sham arm may do better. If the operation adds risk and no benefit, a patient in the real arm is worse off than one in the sham arm. A protocol must say, in advance, what result would stop the trial for that reason, and who would decide.

## Before the Round

- Find the data monitoring arrangements in your precedent trial (from Week 3). Who sat on the committee? How often did it meet?
- Find out what, if anything, your precedent trial's sham arm was offered afterwards.

## In the room

**Tabletop.** A data monitoring committee (DMC) meets three times about a fictional sham-controlled trial of 160 patients. It sees unblinded data. The room does not, until the end.

| Meeting | Enrolled | What the committee sees | Its decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 40 | Serious adverse events: 3 in arm A, 1 in arm B. No outcome data yet. | ? |
| 2 | 80 | Primary outcome at 3 months: arm B slightly better than arm A. Wide uncertainty. | ? |
| 3 | 120 | Arm B still ahead. A surgeon writes to the committee asking to stop the trial because "patients are being denied treatment". | ? |

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0:00 | The committee meets. At each meeting it decides: continue, modify, or stop. |
| 0:40 | The allocation is revealed. |
| 0:50 | The room checks each decision against a stopping rule. Was the rule written before the data, or reconstructed after? |
| 1:10 | Write §9. |

## Stopping rules

| Type | Stops the trial when… | Typical form |
|---|---|---|
| Harm | The procedure arm has clearly more serious adverse events | A pre-set threshold for the difference in events |
| Benefit | One arm is clearly better on the primary outcome early | A strict early threshold, relaxed at later looks |
| Futility | The trial can no longer answer its question | A low conditional probability of a meaningful result |

## Key terms

- **Data monitoring committee.** An independent group that reviews unblinded interim data and advises whether to continue.
- **Interim analysis.** A planned look at the data before the trial ends.
- **Post-trial access.** What participants are offered when the trial ends, in particular the sham arm.

## Protocol log — §9 Stopping rules and post-trial access

State your stopping rules for harm, benefit and futility; who sits on your data monitoring committee; how emergency unblinding works; and what the sham arm is offered, and when. Up to 400 words.

## Check yourself

1. Why should the surgeon who wrote at meeting 3 not be told the interim results?
2. What would your trial do if the sham arm were ahead at the halfway point?
3. What does your post-trial access promise cost, and who pays for it?
